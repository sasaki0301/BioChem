Lesson 3: Functional Groups & Organic Molecules

Functional groups determine the chemical behavior of organic molecules. In biochemistry, understanding functional groups helps predict solubility, reactivity, and biological roles of biomolecules.

1. What are Functional Groups?

A functional group is a specific grouping of atoms within a molecule that imparts characteristic chemical properties and reactivity. Organic molecules are often categorized by the functional groups they contain.

Why they matter: Two molecules with the same carbon skeleton can behave very differently if they have different functional groups (e.g., ethanol vs. ethanoic acid).

3. How Functional Groups Affect Chemical Properties

Functional groups influence:

  • Polarity and solubility — polar groups (e.g., −OH, −COOH) increase water solubility.
  • Acidity/basicity — acidic groups (−COOH, −PO₄H) donate H⁺; basic groups (−NH₂) accept H⁺.
  • Reactivity — carbonyls are electrophilic; thiols can be oxidized to disulfides.
  • Formation of bonds — esters and amides form by condensation (dehydration) reactions.

Examples & short explanations

  • Esterification (condensation): carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester + water. This reaction forms the ester bonds in fats (triglycerides).
  • Peptide bond formation (amide): amino group of one amino acid reacts with carboxyl of another → amide (peptide) + water.
  • Oxidation of thiols: two cysteine residues form a disulfide (—S—S—) — stabilizes tertiary structure of proteins.
Mechanistic note: In many biochemical reactions, enzymes lower the activation energy but the fundamental chemical reactivity still depends on these functional groups.

4. Naming basics & quick rules

Organic nomenclature can be complex but the basics are useful:

  • Identify the longest carbon chain (parent).
  • Number the chain so that functional groups get the lowest possible numbers.
  • Use suffixes for highest priority groups (e.g., −COOH → -oic acid, −OH → -ol, −NH₂ → -amine).

5. Practice Problems (with worked explanations)

Problem 1: Solubility prediction

Which is more water-soluble: butanol (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH) or butanoic acid (CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH)? Explain briefly.

Butanoic acid is generally more polar and can ionize (lose H⁺) in water to form carboxylate (COO⁻), increasing solubility. However, short-chain alcohols are also soluble — for long chains, hydrophobic effects reduce solubility.

Problem 2: Reaction identification

Which functional group transformation occurs when two amino acids join to form a dipeptide?

Formation of an amide (peptide) bond between the carboxyl group (−COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (−NH₂) of another, releasing water (condensation).

Problem 3: Biological example

Explain how phosphate functional groups are important in energy transfer in cells.

Phosphate groups (in ATP) form high-energy bonds between phosphate units. Hydrolysis of these bonds releases energy that drives many cellular processes. The negative charges also help interactions with proteins and enzymes.

6. Key Terms Summary

  • Functional group: Specific group of atoms that give a molecule its chemical properties.
  • Hydroxyl (alcohol): −OH, polar and H-bonding.
  • Carboxyl: −COOH, acidic and ionizable.
  • Amino: −NH₂, basic and proton-accepting.
  • Amide (peptide bond): −CONH−, links amino acids in proteins.
  • Thiol: −SH, can form disulfide bridges (—S—S—).
  • Phosphate: −PO₄²⁻, involved in energy transfer (ATP).
