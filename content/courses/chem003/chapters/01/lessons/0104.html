Chapter 1 Summary: Foundations of Biochemistry

In this chapter, you explored how chemistry forms the basis of all life processes. You learned what biochemistry is, the chemical foundations of life, and how functional groups define the structure and reactivity of biomolecules.

1. What is Biochemistry?

  • Biochemistry is the study of the chemical substances and processes that occur within living organisms.
  • It bridges biology and chemistry to explain life at the molecular level.
  • Key biomolecules include carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
  • Applications: medicine (drug design), food science, genetics, forensics, and molecular biology.

2. Chemical Basis of Life

  • Life is composed mainly of six elements: C, H, O, N, P, and S (the biogenic elements).
  • Atoms form bonds — covalent (sharing), ionic (transfer), and hydrogen (weak attraction).
  • Water, the “molecule of life,” is polar and essential for biochemical reactions.
  • Hydrogen bonding gives water its unique properties (solvent ability, cohesion, high heat capacity).

3. Functional Groups and Organic Molecules

  • Functional groups define a molecule’s reactivity and polarity.
  • Examples include:
    • Hydroxyl (–OH): in alcohols, polar.
    • Carboxyl (–COOH): acidic, in amino and fatty acids.
    • Amino (–NH₂): basic, in proteins.
    • Phosphate (–PO₄²⁻): energy transfer (ATP).
    • Thiol (–SH): forms disulfide bonds in proteins.
  • Functional groups are the foundation of all biochemical reactions and macromolecular structures.

5. Key Terms Summary

  • Biogenic Elements: The six elements essential for life: C, H, O, N, P, S.
  • Functional Group: Atom cluster determining molecular reactivity.
  • Covalent Bond: Electron-sharing bond type.
  • Hydrogen Bond: Weak bond responsible for water’s special properties.
  • Polarity: Uneven charge distribution within a molecule.
  • Macromolecule: Large biological molecules—proteins, lipids, carbs, nucleic acids.
