Big Idea: Proteins are polymers of amino acids whose structure determines their function.

1. Formation of Proteins (Peptide Bonds)

Proteins are formed when amino acids link together through peptide bonds.

  • Condensation reaction
  • Water is released
  • Bond forms between −COOH and −NH₂
Key Idea: Peptide bond = amide linkage
Quick Check: What type of reaction forms peptide bonds?
Condensation reaction

2. Levels of Protein Structure

LevelDescription
PrimaryAmino acid sequence
SecondaryAlpha-helix and beta-sheet
Tertiary3D folding
QuaternaryMultiple chains
Important: Structure determines function.
Quick Check: Which level determines protein identity?
Primary structure

3. Protein Folding and Interactions

Protein folding is driven by interactions between R-groups.

  • Hydrogen bonds
  • Ionic bonds
  • Hydrophobic interactions
  • Disulfide bonds
Hydrophobic Effect: Nonpolar groups cluster away from water.
Quick Check: Which interaction is strongest?
Disulfide bond

5. Denaturation of Proteins

Denaturation is the loss of protein structure due to external factors.

  • Heat
  • pH changes
  • Chemicals
Important: Denaturation affects structure but not primary sequence.
Quick Check: Which structure remains intact during denaturation?
Primary structure
