Chapter 5 Summary: Proteins and Nucleic Acids

1. Amino Acids

  • Building blocks of proteins
  • Contain amino group, carboxyl group, and R-group
  • R-group determines properties
Key Idea: Structure of amino acids determines protein behavior.
Quick Check: What determines amino acid identity?
R-group

2. Protein Structure

LevelDescription

SecondaryHelix/sheet
Tertiary3D folding

Important: Structure determines function.
Quick Check: Which structure is most fundamental?
Primary structure

3. Protein Folding and Interactions

  • Hydrogen bonds
  • Ionic interactions
  • Hydrophobic interactions
  • Disulfide bonds
Hydrophobic Effect: Drives folding by avoiding water.
Quick Check: Which interaction is strongest?
Disulfide bonds

4. Protein Function and Denaturation

  • Enzymes catalyze reactions
  • Proteins perform structural and regulatory roles
  • Denaturation alters structure
Important: Denaturation affects function but not primary structure.
Quick Check: What causes denaturation?
Heat, pH, chemicals

5. Nucleotides and Nucleic Acids

  • Made of phosphate, sugar, base
  • DNA and RNA store genetic information
Quick Check: Which component varies?
Nitrogenous base

7. Base Pairing and DNA Structure

  • A–T (or A–U in RNA)
  • C–G
  • Double helix structure
Important: Complementary base pairing ensures accuracy.
Quick Check: What bonds hold DNA together?
Hydrogen bonds

8. Central Dogma

  • DNA → RNA → Protein
  • Replication, transcription, translation
Big Idea: Genetic information controls protein synthesis.
Quick Check: Which step produces proteins?
Translation

9. Big Picture Connections

  • Amino acids → proteins
  • Proteins → function
  • DNA → instructions
  • RNA → execution
Ultimate Idea: Life is controlled by the flow of information from DNA to proteins.
