Classification and Types of Enzymes

Overview

  • Why enzymes are classified
  • How enzymes are named
  • The major classes of enzymes
  • Examples and functions of important enzymes
  • Medical and biological significance of enzyme classification
Big Idea: Thousands of enzymes exist in living organisms. Scientists classify them according to the specific type of chemical reaction they catalyze.

1. Why Are Enzymes Classified?

Scientists have discovered thousands of enzymes in living organisms. To study them efficiently, enzymes are grouped according to the type of chemical reaction they perform.

This classification system helps scientists predict enzyme functions, understand metabolic pathways, diagnose diseases, and develop medicines.

Analogy: Just as books are organized by subject in a library, enzymes are organized by the reactions they catalyze.
Quick Check: Why is enzyme classification useful to scientists?
Classification helps scientists identify enzyme functions, understand metabolism, and communicate scientific findings consistently.

2. How Are Enzymes Named?

Most enzyme names end with the suffix -ase. Many enzymes are named after the substrate they act upon or the reaction they catalyze.

Substrate Enzyme Function
Starch Amylase Breaks starch into sugars
Lactose Lactase Breaks lactose into simpler sugars
Lipids Lipase Breaks fats into fatty acids and glycerol
Proteins Protease Breaks proteins into amino acids

Some enzymes were named before modern classification systems existed and therefore do not end in -ase.

Examples include pepsin, trypsin, and chymotrypsin.

Quick Check: An enzyme breaks down cellulose. What would its likely name be?
Cellulase, because enzymes are often named after their substrate plus the suffix "-ase".

3. The Major Classes of Enzymes

The International Union of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology (IUBMB) classifies enzymes into six major classes, with a newer seventh class added recently.

The major classes of enzymes are based on the reactions they catalyze.

Class Main Function
Oxidoreductases Transfer electrons
Transferases Transfer functional groups
Hydrolases Break bonds using water
Lyases Add or remove groups without hydrolysis
Isomerases Rearrange atoms within molecules
Ligases Join molecules using ATP
Translocases Transport molecules across membranes

4. Oxidoreductases

Oxidoreductases catalyze oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions involving the transfer of electrons.

These reactions are essential in cellular respiration, photosynthesis, and energy production.

Enzyme Substrate Function Importance
Alcohol Dehydrogenase Alcohol Breaks down alcohol Detoxification in the liver
Lactate Dehydrogenase Lactate Converts lactate ↔ pyruvate Energy metabolism
Catalase Hydrogen peroxide Converts H₂O₂ into water and oxygen Protects cells from damage
Cytochrome Oxidase Electrons and oxygen Final electron transfer step ATP production
Real-Life Connection: Catalase prevents toxic hydrogen peroxide from accumulating in cells.
Quick Check: Why would cells be damaged without catalase?
Hydrogen peroxide would accumulate and damage cellular structures through oxidation.

5. Transferases

Transferases transfer functional groups from one molecule to another.

Enzyme Group Transferred Function Importance
Hexokinase Phosphate Adds phosphate to glucose First step of glycolysis
Alanine Transaminase (ALT) Amino group Amino acid metabolism Liver function testing
Aspartate Transaminase (AST) Amino group Amino acid metabolism Liver function testing
Methyltransferase Methyl group Adds methyl groups to DNA Gene regulation
Medical Application: Elevated ALT and AST levels may indicate liver damage.
Quick Check: Which transferase plays an important role in glycolysis?
Hexokinase, because it transfers a phosphate group from ATP to glucose.

6. Hydrolases

Hydrolases catalyze hydrolysis reactions, breaking chemical bonds by adding water.

Most digestive enzymes belong to this class.

Enzyme Substrate Function Importance
Amylase Starch Produces simple sugars Carbohydrate digestion
Lactase Lactose Produces glucose and galactose Milk digestion
Lipase Fats Produces fatty acids and glycerol Fat digestion
Protease Proteins Produces amino acids Protein digestion
Real-Life Connection: Lactose intolerance occurs when the body produces insufficient lactase.
Quick Check: Why are most digestive enzymes hydrolases?
Because digestion involves breaking large molecules into smaller molecules using water.

7. Lyases

Lyases add or remove groups from molecules without using water or oxidation-reduction reactions.

Enzyme Function Importance
Pyruvate Decarboxylase Removes carbon dioxide Fermentation
Aldolase Splits fructose molecules Glycolysis
Fumarase Converts fumarate ↔ malate Krebs Cycle
Real-Life Connection: Several reactions in glycolysis and the Krebs cycle depend on lyases.
Quick Check: Which lyase helps split molecules during glycolysis?
Aldolase helps split larger sugar molecules during glycolysis.

8. Isomerases

Isomerases rearrange atoms within molecules, converting one isomer into another.

Enzyme Function Importance
Phosphoglucose Isomerase Glucose-6-phosphate ↔ Fructose-6-phosphate Glycolysis
Triose Phosphate Isomerase Interconverts triose phosphates ATP production
Racemase Converts stereoisomers Amino acid metabolism
Why Important? Many metabolic pathways require molecules to be converted into specific forms before they can proceed.
Quick Check: What is the main role of isomerases?
They rearrange atoms within molecules to create different isomeric forms.

9. Ligases

Ligases join molecules together using energy obtained from ATP.

Enzyme Function Importance
DNA Ligase Joins DNA fragments DNA replication and repair
RNA Ligase Joins RNA molecules RNA repair
Aminoacyl-tRNA Synthetase Attaches amino acids to tRNA Protein synthesis
Biotechnology Application: DNA ligase is commonly used in genetic engineering and recombinant DNA technology.
Quick Check: Which ligase is essential during DNA replication?
DNA ligase joins DNA fragments and repairs breaks in DNA strands.

10. Translocases

Translocases transport molecules or ions across biological membranes.

Enzyme Function Importance
ATP Synthase Moves protons and produces ATP Cellular respiration
Sodium-Potassium ATPase Pumps Na⁺ and K⁺ ions Nerve impulses
Calcium ATPase Moves calcium ions Muscle contraction
Real-Life Connection: The sodium-potassium pump is essential for nerve signaling and muscle function.
Quick Check: Why is ATP synthase one of the most important enzymes in biology?
ATP synthase produces ATP, the primary energy currency of cells.

11. Practice Activities

Activity 1: Classification Challenge

A scientist discovers an enzyme that breaks proteins into amino acids using water. Which enzyme class does it belong to? Explain.

It belongs to the hydrolase class because it breaks chemical bonds through hydrolysis using water.

Activity 2: Medical Application

A patient's blood test shows unusually high ALT and AST levels. What organ may be affected and why?

The liver may be damaged because ALT and AST are transferase enzymes commonly released when liver cells are injured.

Activity 3: Metabolism Connection

Why would ATP production decrease if cytochrome oxidase stopped functioning?

Cytochrome oxidase is involved in the electron transport chain. Without it, efficient ATP production would be disrupted.

Activity 4: Higher-Order Challenge

DNA ligase becomes nonfunctional during DNA replication. Predict two consequences for the cell.

DNA fragments would remain unjoined, replication could be incomplete, and mutations or cell death could occur.
