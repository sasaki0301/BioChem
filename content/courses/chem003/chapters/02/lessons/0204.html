Chapter 2 Summary: Hydrocarbon Derivatives and Reactions

In this chapter, you learned how important organic functional groups behave, how they are named, and how they are converted into one another through common organic reactions.

1. Alcohols, Ethers, Thiols, Ketones, and Aldehydes

These functional groups are important because they determine how organic molecules interact with water, proteins, enzymes, and other biological molecules.

Functional Group General Structure Key Idea
Alcohol R−OH Polar; can form hydrogen bonds; can be oxidized.
Ether R−O−R′ Less reactive; oxygen links two carbon groups.
Thiol R−SH Can form disulfide bonds important in protein structure.
Aldehyde R−CHO Terminal carbonyl; easily oxidized to carboxylic acid.
Ketone R−CO−R′ Internal carbonyl; formed from secondary alcohol oxidation.
Important pathway:
Primary alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid

2. Carboxylic Acids, Esters, Amines, and Amides

These groups are especially important in biomolecules such as fatty acids, fats, proteins, amino acids, and nucleic acid bases.

Functional Group General Structure Biochemical Importance
Carboxylic Acid R−COOH Acidic; found in fatty acids and amino acids.
Ester R−COOR′ Found in fats, oils, waxes, and many fragrant compounds.
Amine R−NH₂ Basic; found in amino acids, DNA bases, and neurotransmitters.
Amide R−CONH₂ Forms peptide bonds in proteins; stabilized by resonance.
Key comparison:
Carboxylic acids are acidic, amines are basic, esters are often fragrant, and amides are stable because of resonance.

3. Interconversion and Organic Reactions

Interconversion means transforming one functional group into another. This is important in digestion, metabolism, drug breakdown, and biomolecule synthesis.

Reaction Type Meaning Example
Oxidation Loss of hydrogen, gain of oxygen, or loss of electrons. Alcohol → Aldehyde → Acid
Reduction Gain of hydrogen or electrons. Aldehyde → Alcohol
Condensation Two molecules join while water is removed. Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water
Hydrolysis Water breaks a bond. Ester + Water → Acid + Alcohol

4. Quick Review Checkpoints

Checkpoint 1

Which functional group is responsible for the acidic behavior of fatty acids?

Carboxylic acid group, −COOH.

Checkpoint 2

What reaction type forms esters and peptide bonds?

Condensation reaction, because water is removed.

Checkpoint 3

What reaction breaks esters, fats, or peptide bonds during digestion?

Hydrolysis, because water is used to break bonds.

Checkpoint 4

Why are amides less reactive than esters?

Because amides are strongly stabilized by resonance between nitrogen and the carbonyl group.

5. Key Terms Summary

  • Alcohol: Organic compound with −OH group.
  • Ether: Compound with oxygen between two carbon groups.
  • Thiol: Sulfur-containing group −SH.
  • Aldehyde: Terminal carbonyl group −CHO.
  • Ketone: Internal carbonyl group.
  • Carboxylic Acid: Acidic −COOH group.
  • Ester: Formed from carboxylic acid and alcohol.
  • Amine: Nitrogen-containing basic group.
  • Amide: Stable group found in peptide bonds.
  • Condensation: Joining reaction that releases water.
  • Hydrolysis: Breaking reaction that uses water.
  • Interconversion: Transformation of one functional group into another.
