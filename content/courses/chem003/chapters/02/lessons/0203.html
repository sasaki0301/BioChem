Lesson 3: Interconversion and Reactions of Organic Compounds

Overview

In biochemistry and organic chemistry, molecules are constantly transformed into one another. This process is called interconversion.

Understanding these reactions helps explain:

  • How nutrients are processed in the body
  • How drugs are metabolized
  • How biomolecules are built and broken down
What does "interconversion" mean?
The transformation of one functional group into another.

1. Types of Organic Reactions

A. Oxidation

Oxidation involves loss of electrons, removal of hydrogen, or addition of oxygen.

B. Reduction

Reduction is the opposite: gain of electrons or addition of hydrogen.

Is converting alcohol → aldehyde oxidation or reduction?
Oxidation (loss of hydrogen).

C. Condensation

Two molecules combine and release water.

D. Hydrolysis

Water breaks a molecule into smaller parts.
