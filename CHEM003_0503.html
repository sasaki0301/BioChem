Big Idea: Nucleic acids store and transmit genetic information that controls all cellular processes.

1. What Are Nucleic Acids?

Nucleic acids are biological macromolecules that carry genetic information.

  • DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)
  • RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)
Quick Check: What is the main function of nucleic acids?
To store and transmit genetic information.

2. Structure of Nucleotides

Nucleic acids are made of repeating units called nucleotides.

  • Phosphate group
  • 5-carbon sugar
  • Nitrogenous base
Key Idea: The sequence of bases encodes genetic information.
Quick Check: Which part varies in nucleotides?
Nitrogenous base

3. DNA vs RNA

FeatureDNARNA
SugarDeoxyriboseRibose
StrandsDoubleSingle
BasesA, T, C, GA, U, C, G
FunctionStorageProtein synthesis
Quick Check: Which base is unique to RNA?
Uracil (U)

4. Base Pairing Rules

  • A pairs with T (DNA)
  • A pairs with U (RNA)
  • C pairs with G
Complementary Base Pairing: Ensures accurate replication.
Quick Check: Why is base pairing important?
It ensures accurate copying of genetic information.

5. DNA Structure

DNA has a double helix structure.

  • Two strands
  • Twisted ladder shape
  • Hydrogen bonds between bases
Quick Check: What holds the strands together?
Hydrogen bonds

6. Types of RNA

Quick Check: Which RNA brings amino acids?
tRNA

7. Flow of Genetic Information

Genetic information flows from DNA to RNA to protein.

Central Dogma: DNA → RNA → Protein
  • Replication (DNA copying)
  • Transcription (DNA → RNA)
  • Translation (RNA → protein)
Quick Check: Which step produces proteins?
Translation
