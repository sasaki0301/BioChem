Lesson 1: What is Biochemistry?

Biochemistry is the study of the chemical substances and processes that occur within living organisms. It connects the fields of biology and chemistry to explain how life works at a molecular level.

1. Understanding Biochemistry

Biochemistry explores the structure, composition, and reactions of substances in living things. It focuses on molecules like carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids—all vital for life.

  • It examines how cells harness energy through metabolism.
  • It explains how DNA and RNA store and transmit genetic information.
  • It investigates how enzymes catalyze reactions to sustain life.

Biochemistry links chemical reactions to life processes like energy use, growth, and reproduction.

2. The Role of Biochemistry in Science and Society

Biochemistry helps us understand how life functions at the molecular level. Its discoveries have improved medicine, agriculture, and biotechnology.

It explains life using chemical principles — understanding atoms, molecules, and reactions inside organisms.

3. Major Areas of Biochemistry

  • Structural Biochemistry: Studies the 3D structure of biomolecules like proteins and DNA.
  • Metabolic Biochemistry: Examines how the body uses and converts energy.
  • Genetic Biochemistry: Focuses on how genetic information is stored and expressed.
  • Enzymology: Studies enzyme functions and their role as biological catalysts.

5. Key Terms Summary

  • Biochemistry: The study of chemical processes in living organisms.
  • Metabolism: The sum of all chemical reactions within an organism.
  • Enzyme: A biological catalyst that speeds up chemical reactions.
  • Macromolecules: Large molecules essential for life (proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids).
  • DNA/RNA: Molecules carrying genetic information.
