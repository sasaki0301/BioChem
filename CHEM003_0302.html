Lesson 2: Monosaccharides and Cyclization

1. Review: Monosaccharides

Monosaccharides are the simplest carbohydrates. However, an important detail is often overlooked: they rarely stay in straight-chain form in real biological systems.

Key Idea: Monosaccharides exist mostly in cyclic (ring) form in aqueous solutions.
What form do sugars mostly exist in inside cells?
Cyclic (ring) form, not open-chain.

2. Open-Chain vs Cyclic Form

Monosaccharides can exist in two forms:

  • Open-chain form: straight structure with carbonyl group
  • Cyclic form: ring formed by internal reaction

This transformation happens spontaneously in water.

3. How Cyclization Happens (Step-by-Step)

Cyclization is an intramolecular reaction:

  1. Hydroxyl (−OH) group attacks carbonyl carbon
  2. New bond forms (C−O)
  3. Ring structure is created
Important: This forms either a 5-membered (furanose) or 6-membered (pyranose) ring.

4. Anomers: Alpha and Beta Forms

When the ring forms, a new carbon is created called the anomeric carbon.

This leads to two forms:

  • Alpha (α): OH is below the ring
  • Beta (β): OH is above the ring
What determines alpha vs beta?
Position of OH group on the anomeric carbon.

5. Mutarotation

Sugars constantly switch between α and β forms.

This process is called mutarotation.

6. Biological Importance

  • Enzymes recognize specific forms (α or β)
  • Starch = α glucose
  • Cellulose = β glucose
Same molecule (glucose), different structure → different function.
