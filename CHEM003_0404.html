Chapter 4 Summary: Lipids

1. What Are Lipids?

Lipids are a diverse group of biomolecules that are characterized by their poor solubility in water. Unlike carbohydrates and proteins, lipids are mostly nonpolar.

Key Idea: Lipids are grouped based on behavior (hydrophobicity), not a single structure.
  • Hydrophobic (nonpolar)
  • Some are amphipathic
  • Important in energy storage and membranes
Quick Check: Why are lipids not soluble in water?
Because they are mostly nonpolar and cannot interact strongly with polar water molecules.

3. Fatty Acids

Fatty acids are long hydrocarbon chains with a carboxylic acid group.

  • Saturated: no double bonds
  • Unsaturated: one or more double bonds
Important Trend:
  • More saturation → higher melting point
  • More double bonds → lower melting point
Quick Check: Why are unsaturated fats usually liquid?
Double bonds create bends, preventing tight packing.

4. Triglycerides

Triglycerides are formed from glycerol + 3 fatty acids.

  • Main energy storage molecules
  • Stored in adipose tissue
  • Highly energy-rich
Reaction: Esterification (condensation)
Quick Check: What type of bond connects fatty acids?
Ester bond

5. Complex Lipids

  • Phospholipids: membrane structure
  • Glycolipids: cell recognition
  • Lipoproteins: transport
Quick Check: What makes phospholipids special?
They are amphipathic.

6. Biological Membranes

Cell membranes are made of a phospholipid bilayer.

  • Hydrophilic heads face water
  • Hydrophobic tails face inward
Fluid Mosaic Model: Membrane is flexible and dynamic.
Quick Check: Why is membrane called fluid?
Because molecules can move within the membrane.

8. Key Concepts Summary

  • Lipids are hydrophobic molecules
  • Fatty acids determine properties of lipids
  • Triglycerides store energy
  • Phospholipids form membranes
  • Membranes are selective barriers
Big Picture: Lipids are essential for energy storage, membrane structure, and biological regulation.
