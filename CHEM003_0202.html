Lesson 2: Carboxylic Acids, Esters, Amines, and Amides

Overview

These functional groups are among the most important in biochemistry. They appear in:

  • Fatty acids (carboxylic acids)
  • Fats and oils (esters)
  • Amino acids (amines + carboxylic acids)
  • Proteins (amides/peptide bonds)

1. Carboxylic Acids (−COOH)

Carboxylic acids contain both a carbonyl (C=O) and hydroxyl (−OH) group.

Why are they acidic?

They donate H⁺ and form a carboxylate ion, which is stabilized by resonance.

Why is the carboxylate ion stable?
Resonance spreads the negative charge across oxygen atoms.

2. Esters (−COOR)

Esters are formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol.

This is called esterification.

What type of reaction is esterification?
Condensation (water is removed).

3. Amines (−NH₂)

Amines are basic because nitrogen has a lone pair.

Why are amines basic?
They accept H⁺ using the lone pair.

They are found in amino acids and neurotransmitters.

4. Amides (−CONH₂)

Amides are formed from carboxylic acids and amines.

This forms a peptide bond in proteins.

What makes amides stable?
Resonance between nitrogen and carbonyl group.
